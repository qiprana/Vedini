Getting started

How to Use Vedini

Vedini offers different ways to read, chant, listen, and reflect on the Lalitā Sahasranāma. Start with the experience that suits you, then explore the other tools as you grow comfortable.

1. Full Chanting

Read all 183 main ślokas in order with simple meanings alongside. Switch between Devanagari and Kannada, show or hide IAST and meanings, jump to a verse, and optionally play continuous audio.

Open Full Chanting

2. Study a Śloka

Use the main reader to move between ślokas, read Sanskrit and IAST, and explore word-by-word study and the meanings associated with the divine names.

Open the study reader

3. Listen and Repeat

In the study reader, use the Chant Practice range controls to choose a start and end śloka, repetition count, and singer. You can pause or stop the selected range.

Try Chant Practice

4. Guided Chanting

Learn names with the available MSS recordings. Choose a śloka range, how often to repeat each name, whether to follow with the complete verse, and a practice pause.

Explore Guided Chanting

5. All 1,000 Names

Look up each divine name and its simple meaning and benefit. Search for a word or name, or browse in groups of 200. Benefits are identified as screenshot-sourced or editorial suggestions.

Browse all names

6. Language and Reading

Use the script and meaning language controls available in each reader. Display IAST when you want transliteration, or hide it for a quieter full-chanting view. Print the full-chanting page when you prefer reading on paper.

Choose your reading layout

A simple way to begin

  1. Read a śloka aloud slowly while looking at the Sanskrit and IAST.
  2. Read its simple meaning and notice the names that draw your attention.
  3. Listen to the recording, then repeat in your own voice.
  4. Use continuous chanting when you want to recite without opening each śloka separately.
About recordings: VD and KMP are available for complete-śloka practice. MSS name-level audio is tied to the clips mapped in the study reader; not every name necessarily has a separate recording. Playback requires an internet connection. Meanings and interpretations are for study and contemplation.
